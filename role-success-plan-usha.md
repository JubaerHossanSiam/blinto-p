# Role Success Plan — Usha

## Role

**Frontend Developer**

**Core department:** Engineering  
**Career level:** To be confirmed

## Role purpose

Build dependable, user-friendly frontend experiences from approved requirements and designs with strong attention to quality, responsiveness, maintainability, and delivery commitments.

## Core success outcomes

1. **Implementation accuracy** — completed interfaces match approved design and functional requirements.
2. **Responsive quality** — work functions correctly across expected devices, screen sizes, and supported browsers.
3. **Code quality** — implementation is clear, maintainable, appropriately reusable, and follows team standards.
4. **Self-checking** — obvious visual, interaction, content, and regression issues are found before review or handoff.
5. **Reliable execution** — estimates, progress, blockers, and dependencies are communicated early and tasks are followed through to closure.
6. **Growth in independence** — recurring feedback is applied so similar issues decrease and increasingly complex work can be handled independently.

## Observable evidence

Evidence may include accurate implementation, low preventable rework, responsive and browser quality, code review feedback, successful launches, task reliability, documented learning, and improvement after feedback.

## Role Excellence standard

**Exceptional** performance means Usha consistently produces polished frontend work, catches issues before review, handles increasingly complex work independently, and contributes improvements beyond assigned implementation.

**Effective** performance means assigned frontend work meets requirements, passes expected quality checks, is delivered reliably, and feedback is incorporated into future work.

**Needs Improvement** applies when preventable visual or functional defects recur, testing is weak, rework remains high, blockers are surfaced late, or the same feedback must repeatedly be given.

## Monthly Role Excellence review

Role Excellence should be scored from documented monthly evidence against the outcomes above, not from general impressions. The universal five-level rating scale in the KPI framework applies.
